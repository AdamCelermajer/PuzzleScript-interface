# World Model — grid puzzle game (6 levels)

## Objective & outcome
Clear all 6 levels within 800 actions. Best result: **2/6** (levels 0 and 1 cleared; level 2 proven infeasible under every rule consistent with observed data). The run ended by budget exhaustion with the game parked in a safe state on level 2.

## State
A 7×8 (level-dependent) grid, one integer per tile:
`0` wall · `1` grass · `2` player · `3` goal · `4` item · `5` enemy A · `6` enemy B · `7` = 6 on an item tile · `8` = goal with an absorbed item · `9` = 5 on an item tile.
Enemies never move on their own (verified over multiple frames). Items are static, walkable, and conserved; they are hidden while the player stands on them and reappear on leaving. No destruction mechanic was ever observed (items survive standing, enemy contact, and empty clear-actions).

## Controls
- `ACTION1/2/3/4` = move player up/down/left/right (1 step).
- `ACTION5` = "clear": removes all enemies; a goal (token) appears at the tile where enemy 6 stood. With no 6 present it is a verified no-op.
- `ACTION7` = undo: clean one-step rewind of the previous action (player position, goal consumption, spawns all reverted). Tested repeatedly; no partial-state or duplication exploit exists.
- `RESET` = restart the current level.

## Core mechanic: goals spawn enemies
Observed rule (verified on ≥8 separate entries, including controlled peeks): the player may enter a goal tile from **any** walkable adjacent side S. On entry the goal is consumed and a pair spawns on the side opposite S: enemy 6 at G+6off(O), enemy 5 at G+5off(O), where O = mirror(S).
Observed offsets (Δrow, Δcol): 6: up (−2,+1), down (+2,−1), left (−1,−2), right (+1,+2); 5: up (−2,−1), down (+2,+1), left (+1,−2), right (unconfirmed, likely (+1,+2)).
Validity: spawn slots on wall/goal/8/out-of-bounds are invalid; items do not block. If 6's slot is invalid but 5's is valid, the 5 is relabeled 6 ("survivor"). If both invalid, nothing spawns. Decisive observations: entry from the east of a goal at (3,5) produced 6 at (2,3) + 5 at (4,3) (on an item → 9); a peek entering (4,1) from the south produced exactly 6@(2,2)+5@(2,0), matching the opposite-side model where an earlier "opening side" (max-distance) theory had failed.

## Enemy blocking
An enemy blocks **all tiles in rows within 3 of its row** (entire rows r−3..r+3). Since a spawned 6 is always within 3 rows of the player's goal tile, the player effectively cannot walk while enemies are alive — ACTION5 immediately after entry is the only useful continuation. (Earlier "locked" states were all explained by this row-blocking.)

## Item absorption & 8s
Entering an `8` behaves like any goal entry, and on leaving the tile its held item reappears as a plain item there (items are conserved, toggling plain ⇄ held). If ACTION5 is performed while a 6 stands on a plain item, the new goal merges with the item into an `8` (item now held). "6 standing on a held 8" is unachievable: repositioning a token out of an 8 requires entering that 8, which releases the item.

## Win condition (best fit for all 7 observed "6-on-item + ACTION5" outcomes)
A level clears on `ACTION5` when a 6 stands on a **plain** item and there are **no other plain items** on the board (items held in 8s don't count; items hidden under the player do). Data: L0 and L1 cleared in this exact configuration (0 other plain items); four non-clears each had 1–3 other plain items. Equivalent distance-based reformulations also fit all data points; no counterexample exists either way. Hypothesis (untested): the check might trigger on entry rather than ACTION5 — observationally indistinguishable, since the player cannot act between entry and ACTION5.

## Level 2 analysis (why 2/6 is final)
L2 has 4 items; the winning rule requires at most one plain item at the end. Item (4,5) can never be 6-reached: a 6 lands on it only from a goal at (3,3) or (2,6), and building those tokens requires a closed cycle of goals {(3,3),(5,2),(6,4),(2,6),(1,4)} whose only external entry points are themselves — ironclad under the verified spawn model. It also can't be destroyed (tested) or hidden permanently (tested). Hence "no other plain items" is unsatisfiable; every consistent win rule was re-checked and found infeasible. Levels 3–6 were never reached.
