def push_crate(r,c,d,crates,blue_pass):
    dr,dc=dirs[d]
    cr=set(crates); cr.discard((r,c))
    cur=(r,c); moved=False
    while True:
        nr,nc=cur[0]+dr,cur[1]+dc

        if (nr,nc)==BLUE:
            if not blue_pass:
                return (cr,True,True,cur)   # absorbed, crate destroyed
            else:
                cur=(nr,nc); moved=True; continue
        if not cell_open_base(nr,nc): break
        cur=(nr,nc); moved=True
    if not moved:
        cr.add((r,c)); return None
    cr.add(cur)
    return (frozenset(cr),blue_pass,False,cur)

# World Model — Ice-Sliding Crate Puzzle

## Objective & Outcome
A grid-based sliding puzzle across 6 levels. Each level: move the single **player** so it comes to rest on the **goal** tile. Win a level to advance; finishing all six ends the run. Best outcome achieved: **6/6 levels completed** (task_goal_reached).

## Objects & State
Each cell is one of:
- **Ice** — open floor (value 6); everything slides here.
- **Wall** — any non-floor value (e.g. 0,1,2,3,4,7,8,10,11,12,13,14,20,31,34); immovable, blocks all motion.
- **Goal** (value 5) — the target cell; one per level.
- **Player** (value 9) — the controlled entity; exactly one.
- **Crate** (value 21) — pushable box; several per level.
- **Blue block** (values 22–25) — a solid, "charging" obstacle (see interactions).
- **Gold** (value 27) — passable floor produced from a shattered blue; behaves like ice but is *not* pushable.

State is fully described by: the static grid (walls/goal/blues/gold), the player cell, and the set of crate cells.

## Controls
One action per turn; the grid is returned next turn:
- `ACTION1` = up, `ACTION2` = down, `ACTION3` = left, `ACTION4` = right — slide in that direction.
- `ACTION7` = undo the last move.
- `RESET` = restart the current level (restores crates/blues/goal; the player re-appears at the level start cell).

## Movement & Interactions (core rules)
**Slide.** On a directional action the player moves in that direction, cell by cell, until it would enter a wall — it stops on the last open cell. A goal cell does *not* stop the slide on its own; merely passing over the goal does not win.

**Pushing a crate (the critical rule).** The player does **not** stop in front of a crate. While sliding, when the next cell holds a crate, the player keeps going and *pushes* it. The pushed crate (and any contiguous chain of crates in front of it) slides forward as one group, and the player rides directly behind it. The group advances cell by cell until the lead crate hits a wall or another crate, at which point everything stops and the player rests in the cell immediately behind the lead crate. A push therefore relocates the crate *and* moves the player to a new (often non-stop) cell.

**Shattering a blue.** If the lead crate of a pushed group runs into a blue block, that crate is **destroyed**, the blue is converted to **gold** (becomes passable floor, value 27), and the slide *continues*: the remaining crate(s) and the player carry on in the same direction through the newly opened gold cell until they hit a wall. So a single push can (a) remove a crate, (b) open a passage, and (c) advance the player far past the original blue.
- A crate is only destroyed this way (pushed *into* a blue). 
- The player itself is blocked by a solid blue (a blue is not passable to the player until shattered to gold).
- Gold is passable to both player and crates but cannot itself be pushed or moved.

**Step-by-step example (the L5 win).** 1) Push a crate up out of the way, then push it right into a parking cell. 2) Push a 2-crate chain left into a blue: lead crate shatters, blue→gold, trailing crate continues to a new cell, player lands behind it. 3) Route that new crate to sit directly above a second blue. 4) Push it down: it shatters the second blue (opening a full column), and the player slides down that column to the bottom. 5) Route a *second* crate to the top of that now-open column. 6) Push it down: it travels the open column and stops at the cell below the goal; the player ends exactly **on the goal** → level won.

## Goal & Level Transitions
A level is won when the player's slide **ends** on the goal cell (the goal must act as the player's stopping cell, typically because a crate now blocks the cell just beyond it). On win, the next level loads. The goal is usually *not* a natural slide-stop, so a crate must be positioned to block the cell past it in one of the four approaches.

## Undo / History
