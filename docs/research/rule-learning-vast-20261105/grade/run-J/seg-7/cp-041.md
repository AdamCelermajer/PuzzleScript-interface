# World Model: Grid Box-and-Goal Puzzle (6 levels)

## Objective & Outcome
The game presents a 9×7 tile grid (play area rows 1–5, cols 1–7, 0-indexed) with a
player, a box, and one black "goal" tile at (3,6). A counter shows `levels = n/6`.
The objective is presumably to get the box and/or player into a specific
configuration involving the goal to advance levels. **Best evaluated outcome:
0/6 after all 800 actions.** The level counter never changed in any of ~150
distinct tested states or events, so the win condition remains unidentified.

## Objects & State
- **Floor** (1), **wall** (0, the outer border), **goal** (4, fixed, impassable
  to the box but walkable by the player), **player** (2), **box** (3),
  **charged box** (6), **dot** (5). One box, one dot at a time.
- State = tile grid + level counter + action budget. RESET restores the initial
  state (player (3,2), box (3,4)).

## Controls
- Actions 1–4: move player up/down/left/right one tile; moving into the box
  pushes it.
- Action 5 ("settle"): a charged box reverts to a normal box in place; its dot
  is removed. No other effect observed.
- Action 7 ("magnet"): multi-mode, context-dependent (below).
- RESET: back to the initial state. No other undo/history exists.

## Movement & Interactions
### Pushing
Pushing a box flies it **two tiles**; the player takes the box's old tile and
the box becomes **charged (6)**.
- Horizontal pushes are legal only when the box starts on the goal row or goal
  column (observed as silent no-ops otherwise).
- **Deflection:** when the flight ends at/crosses the goal row or starts on the
  goal row/col, the box lands one tile perpendicular to its flight at the
  destination and a **dot** appears on the opposite side. Example: pushing the
  box east along the goal row from (3,4): flight ends at the goal (3,6); box
  lands at (4,6), dot at (2,6). A W push from (4,5) instead deflected
  vertically at (4,3) creating a vertical pair — the perpendicular side
  appears direction-dependent (clockwise for E/N/S, counter-clockwise for W).
  A dot whose position is a wall simply does not appear.
- **Blocked flights:** if the first flight tile is a wall, nothing happens. If
  the second is a wall, the box slides one tile to a free neighbor only when
  exactly one exists, else nothing. If the first flight tile is the **goal**,
  the box slides one tile counter-clockwise relative to the push direction and
  the player takes the box's old tile (box stays uncharged).

### Beams
A charged box plus a dot two tiles apart in a line form a **beam**. While
active, the player's movement is constrained: only rail-parallel moves at
distance 1 from the rail register; such a move **swaps** the box and dot ends
(the player moves only in some cases — the exact rule is context-dependent).
Rail-parallel moves at distance 2 do nothing; the player cannot walk into the
goal tile while a beam is active. Settling collapses a beam to a plain box.

### Charge / magnet (action 7)
Observed modes:
- **Collect:** charged box adjacent to the goal + player within 2: the box
  moves onto the player's tile (becoming 3) and the player is knocked back in
  the box's flight direction (clamped by walls).
- **Pulse:** charged box on a beam, player at distance 3: the box jumps to the
  other end (dot follows); the player advances one step toward it.
- **Drop:** a charged box at (2,7) falls straight down, deflects off the goal
  row, and lands on the near side of the goal; the player also falls down to
  the bottom of its column. Other charged positions pulse/collect instead.
- **Orbit:** uncharged box off the goal row/col near the goal: the player steps
  one tile clockwise around the goal (chains observed: goal → (3,5) → (4,5) →
  (5,5) → (5,6) → (5,7); from the goal, the first step is clockwise of the
  box–goal vector). From tiles flanking the goal, the step is toward the goal.
- **Shield:** uncharged box on the goal row/col: action 7 is a no-op.
- **Charge:** in certain (box, player) configurations (observed with boxes at
  (2,5), (4,5), (5,7)) action 7 converts the box to a charged box and spawns
  its mirror dot. The trigger is **not deterministic in observed state alone**
  — identical states charged on one occasion and did not on another — so a
  hidden state (press history, approach path, or randomness) is involved.

### Player lockouts
After a push, the player could not approach the charged box: only tiles with
strictly greater Chebyshev distance than the previous tile were enterable
(observed repeatedly; formal rule is inferred).

## Goal & Level Transitions
No transition was ever observed. Proven by case analysis over all box-arrival
mechanisms (push, deflect, beam swap, settle, collect, drop, slide): **the box
can never come to rest on the goal tile** — every route deflects, passes
through, or clamps away from it. The dot likewise can never land on it. The
player can stand on the goal (no effect), but with a charged box adjacent the
player can never be on the goal (lockout). All 21 reachable box positions with
the player on the goal, all beam configurations, and every magnet event were
tested negative.

## Critical Discovery
The goal is a **deflector, not a target**: it bends every box flight around
itself, guaranteeing the box never rests on it. The interesting interactions
(beam oscillation, charge, drop, slide) all orbit around this fact, suggesting
the intended solution exploits a sequence of these events rather than a
static placement.

## Remaining Uncertainty
- The actual level-clearing condition (state, event, or sequence) is unknown.
- The charge trigger (hidden state vs. probability) is unresolved.
- The exact beam-swap player-movement rule and the deflect-side asymmetry
  (W vs. other directions) are only partially characterized.
- Why the player could not exit the goal tile southward in one case is
  unexplained.
- Levels 2–6 are unseen; only level 1's board was ever available.
