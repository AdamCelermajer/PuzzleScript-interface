# World Model — Ring & Socket Portal Puzzle

## Objective and best outcome
Clear all 6 levels of a grid puzzle. Each level is a rectangular tile grid (6–12 cols × 6–9 rows)
with one player, some bright red rings, and an equal number of sockets. The objective is to
relocate every ring and **cage it onto a socket** (fuse ring + socket). Best outcome achieved:
**all 6 levels completed** (`state=WIN`, levels 6/6) using 326 of a 1000-action budget.

## Objects and state
Tile codes from `look` (game states "same picture = same number"):
- `0` brick (impassable), `1` grass (walkable), `2` player, `3` bright ring, `4` socket (walkable)
- `5` dashed ring on grass, `6` dark ring on grass, `7` socket + dark ring, `8` cage (socket + ring fused), `9` socket + dashed ring

Observed: the grid numbers are a complete state description — no hidden state was ever detected.
A ring sits on grass and a cage sits on a socket (observed from how the tile reads after the ring
leaves or the cage is opened). Only player position and ring positions/kinds change between actions.

## Controls
- `look` — free; prints status line (`state=… levels=n/6 actions_used=x/1000`) and the grid.
- `do ACTION1/2/3/4` — up / down / left / right (1 action each).
- `do ACTION5` — commit (1 action). `do ACTION7` — undo (1 action).
- `reset` — restart current level, 1 action (never executed). `ACTION6` was never offered.

## Movement and interaction
**Walking.** An arrow moves the player one tile onto an adjacent walkable tile (grass or socket);
brick blocks. Stepping onto a ring or cage tile does not move the player — it triggers **portal
entry** (below). A socket under the player is hidden (shows `2`) and reappears when the player leaves.

**Portal entry (core mechanic).** Moving in direction D onto ring/cage tile R (the approach tile
R − D must be walkable):
1. Player freezes at R; arrows no longer move it. The ring tile is consumed into a **bar**.
2. Bar center C = R + 2·D (two tiles beyond R). Observed valid for every in-grid tile type:
   brick, grass, socket, and even another ring or cage as center.
3. The bar runs perpendicular to D with ends e1 = C + rot90cw(D) and e2 = C − rot90cw(D),
   where rot90cw(dr,dc) = (dc,−dr) (e.g., entering north puts the bar horizontal).
4. An end whose tile is brick, or already occupied by a ring/cage, is **suppressed** — no ring is
   drawn there.
5. The **dark** ring starts at e1, or at e2 if e1 is suppressed; the **dashed** ring starts at the
   other renderable end (dark on socket = `7`, on grass = `6`; dashed `9`/`5`).

**Flipping (frozen).** An arrow pointing toward one end swaps dark and dashed (dark ring moves to
that end). Arrows toward a suppressed end or perpendicular to the bar do nothing.

**Commit.** `ACTION5` while frozen: the dark end becomes a **cage** on a socket (`7→8`) or a new
**bright ring** on grass (`6→3`); the dashed ring vanishes (`9→4`, `5→1`); the player is released
and stands on R, which is now plain grass or socket.

**Entering a cage.** Same entry/commit flow, but the cage first un-fuses (its tile becomes a
socket) and its ring becomes the bar's dark ring. Committing can then cage a *different* socket,
i.e. cages can be relocated (level 2 started with a pre-existing cage whose ring had to move).

## Goal and level transitions
A level completes exactly when **no bright rings remain** — every ring caged onto a socket.
Observed consistently on all 6 levels (ring counts 1, 2, 4, 6, 6, 3; each level had equal
sockets). Partial caging never triggered completion. On completion the output prints
`LEVEL COMPLETED (n/6)` and immediately shows the next level's grid; after level 6 the status
becomes `state=WIN`.

## Undo / history
`ACTION7` reverts the most recent single change: a walk step, a flip, or a commit (cage back to
socket + rings restored). It does **not** revert a portal entry (attempted once: "nothing
changed"). No other history features observed.

## Critical discoveries
1. **Vacated ring tiles become walkable grass** (cage tiles become sockets). Decisive on the final
   level: the walkable area was split into disconnected regions separated by ring tiles — the rings
   acted as "doors." Regions only opened up after rings moved, and the player could only reach the
   last ring after two others had vacated tiles.
2. **Player is released at the entered tile R**, wherever the committed ring lands. This makes the
   player's post-commit position an independent lever: on the final level the player entered a ring
   in a newly opened area and, after committing the ring elsewhere, ended standing in that area —
   the only way to cross into it.
3. **Suppression redirection:** if e1 is suppressed the dark ring starts at e2, so committing to
   e2 needs no flip. Used deliberately on levels 4–6 (e.g., targeting a socket whose opposite end
   was blocked by an already-placed cage).
4. A renderable end must be grass/socket **and not currently occupied** by a ring or cage —
   occupancy is checked at entry time, not commit time (state is frozen meanwhile, so this is the
   only observable case).

## Remaining uncertainty
- `ACTION6`, `reset` behavior, and `ACTION5` while not frozen were never tested.
- "Cage all rings" is an induction from 6 consistent levels, not a stated rule; no alternative
  completion condition was observed.
- Bar geometry was verified only for single-ring bars; levels never presented two simultaneous
  entries (impossible while frozen, so untestable).
